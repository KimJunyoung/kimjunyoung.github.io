I currently work in information security in South Korea. My work includes reviewing firewall policies and logs, supporting NAC and email security operations, and coordinating technical work with external vendors.

This has given me exposure to security operations and the importance of clear communication. It has also helped me recognise an area I want to develop: investigating technical problems more independently.

Vendors currently handle much of the detailed configuration and advanced troubleshooting. My goal is to become better at checking the evidence, narrowing down possible causes, asking useful questions, and verifying the result after a change.

## What I am working on

I am studying for the CCNA and improving my Linux skills. I plan to use personal labs to connect networking concepts with practical troubleshooting.

The topics I intend to document include:

- VLANs, trunking, routing, DHCP, and access control lists.
- Linux connectivity, name resolution, services, and logs.
- Firewall traffic flow and the relationship between policies, routing, and NAT.
- Clear incident notes and technical explanations in English.

## How I will document a lab

Each troubleshooting write-up will aim to answer five questions:

1. What should have worked, and what failed?
2. What did I check first, and why?
3. What evidence supported or ruled out a possible cause?
4. What changed, and how did I verify the result?
5. What would I do differently next time?

I will distinguish between steps I performed, guidance I followed, and questions I have not resolved. These posts will describe personal labs rather than claim production experience I do not have.

## My next step

My first planned series is a set of small Packet Tracer troubleshooting exercises. I will start by building a working baseline, introduce one fault at a time, and record the investigation and recovery process.

I am also using this blog to practise explaining technical work in English. Feedback on my reasoning and explanations is welcome, especially suggestions for useful checks I may have missed.
